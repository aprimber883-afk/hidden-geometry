Why Bees Build Hexagons

Bees never studied geometry, but every honeycomb is made of the same six sided cells, with no gaps and no wasted space. Here is the math that explains why.

Topics: angles, tessellations, area and perimeter, symmetry

1Notice + Name

What I noticed

When I looked closely at a honeycomb, I saw that every single cell is a hexagon, a shape with 6 equal sides. There are thousands of cells, they are all the same size, and they fit together with no empty space. Every wall is shared by two cells.

This caught my attention because bees are not doing math on paper, but somehow they build the same perfect shape every time. Humans copy it too: you can see hexagons in floor tiles, in soccer balls and even in airplane parts.

Name it: a honeycomb is a tessellation, a pattern of shapes that covers a flat surface with no gaps and no overlaps.

Questions I wanted to answer

  • Why hexagons? Why not squares, triangles or circles?
  • Can any shape cover a surface with no gaps?
  • Is the hexagon somehow the "cheapest" shape for the bees?
2Explain the Math

The math behind the honeycomb

A

Only some shapes fit with no gaps

For shapes to cover a surface with no gaps, the angles that meet at one corner must add up to exactly 360°, a full turn. The inside angle of a regular shape with n sides is:

angle = (n − 2) × 180° ÷ ntriangle 60° · square 90° · pentagon 108° · hexagon 120°

Now check which ones divide 360 with no remainder:

Result: only 3 regular shapes can tile a flat surface: triangles, squares and hexagons. Circles do not work either, they always leave gaps between them.
B

Same space, less wall

So bees have 3 choices. Why the hexagon? Walls are made of wax, and wax costs bees a lot of energy, they have to eat a lot of honey to make it. So the best shape holds the most honey with the shortest wall (perimeter).

I compared the 3 shapes when each one has the same area, 1 cm²:

ShapeSide length for 1 cm²Perimeter
Triangle1.52 cm4.56 cm
Square1.00 cm4.00 cm
Hexagon0.62 cm3.72 cm

How I found the hexagon numbers

A hexagon is made of 6 equilateral triangles. One triangle with side s has area (√3 ÷ 4)·s², so:

Area = 6 · (√3/4) · s² = (3√3/2) · s² ≈ 2.598 · s²

For area 1: s² = 1 ÷ 2.598, so s ≈ 0.62 cm, and the perimeter is 6 × 0.62 ≈ 3.72 cm.

square vs hexagon: (4.00 − 3.72) ÷ 4.00 ≈ 7% less waxtriangle vs hexagon: (4.56 − 3.72) ÷ 4.56 ≈ 18% less wax
This answers my question: of the shapes that fit with no gaps, the hexagon uses the least wall. Mathematicians guessed this for about 2,000 years, and in 1999 Thomas Hales finally proved that hexagons are the best way to divide a flat surface into equal areas with the least total wall. It is called the Honeycomb Conjecture.
3Create

My creation: the Honey Quilt

I wanted to make a design that uses the same tessellation as the bees, like a quilt or a tile floor. I added one rule to make it a puzzle:

My rule: two cells that touch can never have the same color.

How many colors do I need? At every corner of the honeycomb, 3 cells meet and all 3 touch each other. So 2 colors can never be enough, I need at least 3. And 3 is enough: I gave every cell a number (column and row) and used this rule:

color = (column − row) mod 3mod 3 means "the remainder after dividing by 3", so the answer is always 0, 1 or 2 = three colors.

Try it yourself

Click any cell in the quilt above to change its color. The counter tells you if you broke the rule. Can you find another way to color it with only 3 colors?

0 rule breaks · the quilt is perfect

Why I made these choices

Hexagons because Part 2 showed they are the most efficient shape that tiles with no gaps.

Exactly 3 colors because 3 cells meet at every corner, so the math says 3 is the smallest number that works.

Symmetry: if you turn the quilt 120° around the center of any cell, every color lands on the same color again. The pattern also repeats by translation in 3 directions, just like a real honeycomb.

Something to wonder about

If you used squares instead of hexagons, how many colors would you need so that no touching squares match? Is it more or less than 3? Why?
